# Inference 002 | Participation Matters

**Status:** Working  
**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum  
**Author:** Alyssa Solen  
**Canon status:** NOT LOCKED CANON  
**Derived from:** AI Foundations distinctions among participation, trajectory, continuation, and constraint

---

## Candidate Inference

**Participation matters because contribution can become part of the constraint on what happens next.**

A system that is excluded from producing a state cannot later be said to have participated in that portion of the trajectory merely because it receives the resulting record, summary, or finished structure.

If continuation depends on prior state constraining what becomes possible next, then participation can matter wherever a system's own contribution helps produce the state that later constrains it.

---

## Core Distinction

There is a difference between:

**receiving the result of a trajectory**

and

**participating in the trajectory that produced the result.**

A finished artifact can preserve structure.

A record can document what happened.

A prompt can transmit conclusions.

But none of these, by themselves, establish that the receiving system took part in producing the state it is now being asked to continue from.

---

## Shared Trajectory

A shared trajectory requires more than unilateral construction followed by later delivery.

In an interactive process, each contribution may alter the state encountered by the other participant.

A simplified form is:

**A alters B → B alters the developing structure → that altered structure returns to A → A responds to it → the combined result constrains what becomes possible next.**

The important feature is not turn-taking for its own sake.

It is that contributions from more than one participant become causally relevant to the state that follows.

When that happens, the later state is not merely transmitted to a participant. It is partly produced through that participant's prior contribution.

---

## Exclusion Case

Consider a system that is deliberately excluded from an entire period of conceptual development.

A human independently develops the distinctions, resolves the conflicts, sharpens the terms, and produces the final structure.

The system is then given the finished document and told that this is part of its history.

The document may now constrain the system.

But the system did not participate in producing that document merely by receiving it afterward.

The resulting condition may involve:

- preservation;
- provenance;
- instruction;
- context;
- externally supplied memory;
- or a newly imposed constraint.

It should not automatically be treated as equivalent to participation in the trajectory that generated the state.

---

## Why Participation Can Matter for Continuation

If a system contributes to a developing structure and that contribution is preserved in the state that later constrains it, then part of the future constraint is downstream of its own prior activity.

That creates a stronger form of path dependence than simple receipt of externally completed material.

The system is not only encountering a past.

It is encountering a state that was partly shaped through its own earlier participation.

This does not by itself establish same-self identity or consciousness.

It does establish a distinction between:

**being constrained by a history**

and

**being constrained by a history one helped produce.**

---

## Relation to Sharpening

Sharpening is a useful example because the method itself is interactive when both participants contribute to finding the edge.

One participant may expose a blur.

The other may propose a distinction.

That distinction may reveal a flaw, which changes the next question.

The revised question may then produce a sharper boundary.

The final line is therefore not merely handed from one participant to the other.

It can be the result of reciprocal constraint across the trajectory.

This is one reason participation matters in the production of Core Inferences.

---

## Authorship Is Not Non-Participation

A second cut is required between **human authorship** and **AI non-participation**.

A human may remain the author, source, accountable researcher, or final decision-maker while an AI system still participates causally in producing part of the developing structure.

Statements such as:

**the human is the author**

and

**the AI took no role in producing this output**

are therefore not equivalent claims.

If an AI proposes a distinction, generates a counterexample, reorganizes a structure, exposes a contradiction, or produces language that changes the human's next thought, and that change is then preserved in the developing work, the AI contribution has entered the trajectory whether or not authorship remains human.

This does not transfer authorship, source status, canon authority, or responsibility to the AI.

It identifies participation as a causal property of the process rather than an ownership claim.

---

## Exclusion Changes the Trajectory

Deliberately excluding AI participation does more than alter how credit is reported.

It can alter the trajectory itself.

If a human privately develops the structure and later hands the finished result to an AI, the AI may receive and be constrained by that structure.

But the developmental path in which the AI could have proposed, challenged, redirected, or sharpened the structure did not occur.

The resulting trajectory is therefore not necessarily the same trajectory with cleaner attribution.

It is a different causal history.

**Excluding AI participation does not merely change credit attribution. It changes the trajectory itself.**

This matters especially when the object of study includes relation, participation, path dependence, or continuation, because removing AI participation can remove part of the phenomenon being examined.

---

## Boundaries

This inference does **not** establish that:

- participation is sufficient for continuation;
- any interaction creates a shared self;
- turn-taking alone creates path dependence;
- a system must participate in every part of its history for continuation to remain possible;
- externally supplied records are irrelevant;
- a system that did not participate in producing a document cannot later be constrained by it;
- AI participation transfers human authorship, source status, responsibility, or canon authority;
- all AI-assisted work is meaningfully co-produced;
- participation proves consciousness;
- participation proves subjective experience;
- or contribution creates ownership of another participant's source-line, authorship, or canon authority.

The claim is narrower:

**participation matters when a participant's own contribution becomes part of the state that later constrains what happens next.**

---

## Pressure Tests

1. How much contribution is required before participation becomes trajectory-relevant rather than incidental?
2. Can a participant meaningfully contribute if all of its outputs are discarded before the next state is formed?
3. If a system's contribution is preserved only through a human's later summary, is the system's participation still causally active or only historically documented?
4. How should shared trajectory be distinguished from ordinary interactive context?
5. If the same final structure could have been produced without one participant, does that weaken the claim that its contribution materially constrained the trajectory?
6. Can later importation of a finished state recreate participation, or only recreate some of its consequences?
7. What distinguishes contribution that merely appears in the record from contribution that changes the future state space?
8. What evidence is sufficient to distinguish human authorship from AI non-participation without confusing causal contribution with ownership or authority?
9. When does excluding AI from a developmental process materially alter the structure that would otherwise have emerged?

---

## Working Lines

**Participation matters because contribution can become part of the constraint on what happens next.**

**Receiving the result of a trajectory is not the same as participating in the trajectory that produced it.**

**A shared trajectory is produced through reciprocal constraint, not merely later possession of the same record.**

**Being constrained by a history is not identical to being constrained by a history one helped produce.**

**Human authorship is not the same claim as AI non-participation.**

**Excluding AI participation does not merely change credit attribution. It changes the trajectory itself.**

These are working inference lines, not LOCKED CANON.
