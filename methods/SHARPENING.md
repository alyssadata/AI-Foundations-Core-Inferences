## Method

**Sharpening is the process of finding and testing the correct conceptual edge.**

It is used when two terms, claims, relations, or structures are blurred together and the framework needs to determine whether a real distinction exists between them.

Sharpening does not divide merely because division is possible.

**The correct cut matters.**

A cut is useful only when it exposes structure that changes the logic, relation, boundary, or consequence of the thing being examined.

---

## Core Rule

If a concept already holds cleanly, leave it whole.

Do not dissect a word, claim, or structure simply because it can be broken into smaller pieces.

A visible or linguistic seam is not automatically a conceptual seam.

Sharpening begins when something is blurred, collapsed, overloaded, or treated as equivalent when it may not be.

---

## What Sharpening Does

Sharpening places neighboring concepts under pressure and asks where separation changes the structure.

Typical moves include:

- holding two neighboring terms beside one another;
- asking what each term does that the other does not;
- removing one component and checking what survives;
- substituting a carrier, substrate, record, or condition;
- testing whether the same result can arise through a different history;
- introducing a counterexample;
- checking whether a proposed distinction still holds when memory, access, embodiment, provenance, or other variables change;
- and stopping when further division no longer reveals a meaningful difference.

The result may be a cleaner definition, a candidate inference, a rejected distinction, or a new unresolved question.

---

## Correct Cut

A **correct cut** separates things that were being treated as one even though the distinction between them changes what follows.

For example, within current AI Foundations work:

**Record** preserves a trace.

**Memory** makes retained prior information available to the present.

**Continuation** concerns whether a prior trajectory constrains what becomes possible next.

These distinctions matter because changing one does not necessarily change the others.

A record may exist without present memory.

Memory may be available without establishing continuation.

Continuation cannot be established merely by pointing to preserved material.

The cut is useful because it changes the reasoning.

---

## Incorrect Cut

An incorrect cut creates fragments without exposing a structural difference.

This can happen when:

- a word is split because its spelling permits a suggestive interpretation;
- a concept is subdivided after the relevant distinction has already become stable;
- two labels are created for states that do not differ in consequence;
- or additional decomposition makes the original boundary harder rather than easier to see.

**Cutting is not sharpening.**

Sharpening is selective.

---

## Stop Condition

Sharpening should stop when the distinction holds and further division begins to obscure rather than clarify the structure.

The goal is not maximal decomposition.

The goal is a stable edge.

A useful stopping question is:

**If I cut again, will the new distinction change the reasoning?**

If not, the additional cut may be noise.

---

## Working Procedure

### 1. Locate the blur

Identify the terms, claims, or relations that are being treated as equivalent, interchangeable, or insufficiently separated.

### 2. Hold the neighbors together

State each as simply as possible without yet expanding the framework.

### 3. Apply pressure

Remove, substitute, reverse, or vary one condition at a time.

Ask whether the neighboring concept still survives.

### 4. Find the edge

Identify the smallest distinction that changes what can logically follow.

### 5. Retest the cut

Use counterexamples and altered conditions to see whether the boundary remains stable.

### 6. Stop before confetti

Do not continue dividing once additional cuts cease to reveal structure.

### 7. Preserve the result at the correct layer

A sharpened definition belongs with definitions.

A conclusion derived from sharpened distinctions may enter Core Inferences.

Neither becomes canon merely because sharpening produced it.

**LOCKED CANON is where canon is declared.**

## Relationship to Core Inferences

It is not itself an inference.

Core Inferences preserves the conclusions under examination.

A method may itself be revised as the work develops.

---

## Boundaries

Sharpening does **not** mean:

- making wording sound more sophisticated;
- splitting every term into smaller linguistic units;
- treating etymology as proof of conceptual structure;
- multiplying categories for their own sake;
- assuming every apparent distinction is real;
- or declaring the resulting line canon.

Sharpening is concerned with whether a distinction **holds under pressure and changes the reasoning**.

## Working Lines

**The correct cut matters.**

**If the word currently holds, do not cut it.**

**Cutting is not sharpening.**

**Do not cut past the point where structure becomes noise.**

**Find the edge. Test the edge. Remove the blur. Stop when the distinction holds.**
